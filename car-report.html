Car Report: what a used car should really cost

Feed it the listings for any make and model and it produces a market report and an interactive price calculator, in English or Spanish. KerbsideCars, the live version, compares models side by side.

By Nicolás Martelli · 4 min read

At a glance

What
Used-car price reports and a calculator for any make and model in Australia
My role
Data analysis and build
Stack
Python, pandas, scikit-learn, SQLite, matplotlib, ReportLab (PDF), static HTML and JavaScript
Status
Working tool with a live demo, KerbsideCars

1. Is this price fair?

Online asking prices for the same car can differ by thousands of dollars, and it is hard to tell a bargain from a bad buy. Car Report turns a few hundred listings into a clear answer: what a car like yours typically sells for, what drives the price, and which listings are worth a look.

2. What it produces

  • A PDF report in English or Spanish: a market summary, charts, what moves the price (year, kilometres, transmission, version) and the five best-value listings.
  • A price calculator that opens with a double click, no server needed: your car against the market, the closest listings with links, and a forecast of its value over the next three years.
  • A market explorer with filters, showing every listing and how far its price sits from the typical one.
  • KerbsideCars, one page with Compare, Price calculator and Explore for every model with enough listings.

3. The pricing model

The typical price comes from a regression on the log of the price, using year, kilometres, transmission, keywords from the listing title (which carry the version) and, when given, the state. Working in logs means each extra year or 10,000 km costs a percentage of the price, not a fixed amount, which is how the market behaves.

Every model is tested on listings it has not seen, with cross-validation repeated over several splits, and the report states its typical error. For a common model it lands around 4%. The report is also clear about its limits: these are asking prices, not sale prices, and a version the title does not mention can throw a price off, so those listings are flagged.

4. The data

  • Collection: listings are gathered from Gumtree by a browser agent driven by Claude, slowly enough to respect the site's rate limits.
  • No personal data: no seller names, phone numbers, addresses, plates or VINs are stored.
  • A history, not a snapshot: each collection is added to a SQLite database with one row per listing per collection, so loading the same file twice never duplicates anything.
  • Respecting terms: where a site allows links but not reuse of its data, the tool only links to it.

5. Try it

KerbsideCars is the live version, built from real listings collected in Western Australia.
